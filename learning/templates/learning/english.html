THE TOOLS OF ENGLISH

Key ideas

01

Reading means making sense of words and thinking about the ideas they share.

02

Writing organises your ideas so another person can follow them.

03

Vocabulary is the collection of words you know and choose from.

04

Punctuation helps show where ideas pause, finish, or ask a question.

BE A TEXT DETECTIVE

How to understand what you read

NOTICE · INFER · FIND EVIDENCE

Start by asking what the text is mostly about. Then notice details that support your thinking. Sometimes a writer does not say everything directly; you can make an inference by combining clues in the text with what you already know. Use a specific detail as evidence for your idea.

MAIN IDEA

What is mostly happening?

Look for an idea that connects several details, rather than focusing on just one small fact.

INFERENCE

What can you work out?

Clues: “Ari zipped his coat and hurried inside as the first drops fell.” Ari may be trying to get out of the rain.

EVIDENCE

Which words support it?

“The first drops fell” is a clue for rain; “hurried inside” suggests Ari wants shelter.

VOCABULARY

Use nearby clues

If an unfamiliar word appears, read the surrounding sentence for hints about its meaning.

YOUR TURNWhat can you infer from this scene?+

“The match was almost over. Sam kept glancing at the scoreboard and bouncing one knee.”

One possible answer: Sam may feel nervous or excited. The repeated glances and bouncing knee are clues, but the text does not tell us for certain.

MAKE YOUR IDEAS EASY TO FOLLOW

How to build a clear paragraph

01 / POINT

Say your main idea

Let the reader know what the paragraph is about.

02 / DETAIL

Add an example

Include a fact, description, or moment that supports your point.

03 / EXPLAIN

Show why it matters

Connect your detail back to your main idea.

04 / CHECK

Read it again

Check spelling, punctuation, and whether the ideas are in a helpful order.

A SHORT EXAMPLE

Point: A school garden can be a useful place to learn.
Detail: Students can watch seeds grow and measure the plants each week.
Explain: This connects science observations with maths and careful writing.

EXTRA THINGS TO KNOW

Words do a lot of work

01

A noun names a person, place, thing, or idea. A verb tells what happens or what something is.

02

An adjective describes a noun; an adverb can describe how an action happens.

03

The same story can sound funny, serious, or mysterious depending on the writer's word choices.

04

Reading different kinds of texts is a good way to meet new words and writing styles.

✦

Remember: good readers look for clues, and good writers organise ideas for their reader.
